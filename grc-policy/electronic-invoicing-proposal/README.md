# Internal Proposal: Electronic Invoicing Conversion

**Course:** Writing for Business (ENGL 4091) · **Term:** Spring 2026 · **Type:** Individual assignment · **Score:** 15/15

📄 **[Read the full proposal →](./proposal.md)**

## Overview
This internal business proposal recommends a two-stage plan to modernize the invoicing process at a small manufacturing company. As the fictional office manager, I identified three operational problems, researched solutions, built a cost model, and wrote a formal proposal to executive leadership requesting approval.

The proposal follows standard business communication structure: a clear problem statement, a phased solution, a financial analysis with a comparison table and chart, qualitative benefits, a timeline, and a closing recommendation.

## The Problem
The accounting department's invoicing workflow had three compounding issues:
- A slow, jam-prone printer caused **180 minutes of lost staff time per week**
- **1,000 paper invoices mailed monthly** at $600/month ($7,200/year)
- A 20-year-old flatbed scanner required **10 minutes per invoice** to generate PDFs

## The Solution
**Stage 1:** Lease a high-speed all-in-one printer ($150/month, 6-month term) and begin converting customers to PDF invoicing, targeting 80% adoption within three months.

**Stage 2:** Upgrade the accounts receivable system to generate and deliver invoices digitally without printing.

## Financial Analysis

| Month | % on PDF | Plan Total | Current Cost | Savings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | 0% | $750 | $600 | -$150 |
| Month 2 | 20% | $630 | $600 | -$30 |
| Month 3 | 40% | $510 | $600 | +$90 |
| Month 4–6 | 80% | $270 | $600 | +$330/mo |
| **6-Month Total** | | **$2,700** | **$3,600** | **+$900** |

The plan costs slightly more in months 1–2 but becomes cost-positive by month 3 and generates $900 in net savings over six months, with continued savings beyond as adoption grows.

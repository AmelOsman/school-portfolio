# Proposal for Electronic Invoicing Conversion

**Great Slate, Inc., Castleton, Vermont**

**TO:** President; Vice President/CFO; Vice President/COO
**FROM:** Amel Osman, Office Manager
**DATE:** April 14, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Electronic Invoicing Conversion: Stage 1 Approval Requested

Over the past several weeks, I have observed that a significant portion of accounting staff time is spent managing printing delays rather than core financial responsibilities. The department's primary printer supports all invoicing tasks but experiences frequent jams, averaging twelve incidents per week and requiring approximately 180 minutes of staff time weekly to resolve. The combination of these printing inefficiencies, growing demand for electronic invoices, and ongoing mailing costs presents a clear opportunity to improve workflow and reduce costs. To address this, I propose a two-stage solution: leasing a modern all-in-one printer, copier, scanner, and fax machine while transitioning to electronic PDF invoicing, followed by upgrading the accounts receivable system to generate and deliver invoices automatically.

## Background and Problem Situation

### Printing Challenges

Great Slate's invoicing process is constrained by an aging, inefficient printing system. The department relies on a single shared printer that produces documents at only 6 pages per minute and experiences an average of 12 jams per week. Each jam takes approximately 15 minutes to resolve, resulting in about 3 hours of lost productivity per week. This printer has become a critical bottleneck, contributing to workflow disruptions and increased stress, particularly following recent downsizing.

### Postage and Mailing Costs

The current process requires invoices to be physically printed before they are recorded in accounts receivable, resulting in ongoing mailing and postage costs. Each month, approximately 1,000 invoices are mailed at a combined cost of $600, or $7,200 annually.

### PDF Scanning Inefficiencies

The company relies on a 20-year-old flatbed scanner that scans one page at a time, requiring multi-page invoices to be scanned individually and manually compiled. This process takes approximately 10 minutes per invoice. As more customers request electronic invoices each month, this workflow increases the burden on staff without improving efficiency.

## Proposed Solution

### Stage 1: Equipment Lease and Customer Conversion

I recommend leasing the Brother MFC-L5915DW Business Monochrome Laser All-in-One Printer at $150 per month for a six-month term. This machine prints at up to 50 pages per minute, supports duplex scanning via a 70-sheet automatic document feeder, and provides direct scan-to-PDF and email functionality.

Customer outreach will begin immediately. Based on available data, approximately 20 percent of customers are expected to convert within the first month, 40 percent within two months, and up to 80 percent within three months.

### Stage 2: Accounting System Upgrade

A longer-term solution will involve upgrading the accounts receivable system so that invoices can be generated as PDF files and delivered automatically to customers, eliminating the need to print before recording.

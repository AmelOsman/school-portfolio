# Ukraine: Resiliency Under Fire — A Cyber Strategy Analysis

## Overview
After studying the cyber strategies of the United States, Russia, and China, I chose a fourth country to analyze: **Ukraine**. My paper argues that Ukraine's cyber strategy is fundamentally different from the major powers because it is driven by **survival rather than the pursuit of advantage**, and that Ukraine uses **transparency as a defensive tool** where the major powers rely on deterrence or secrecy.

## Key Arguments
- **Attacks shaped the strategy.** A decade of Russian-linked attacks, including the 2014 election intrusions, the 2015 BlackEnergy power grid blackout, and the 2017 NotPetya attack, forced Ukraine to build its national cyber defense institutions, such as CERT-UA and the State Cyber Protection Center.
- **Public-private and international partnership.** Ukraine's defense depends on close cooperation among government, private security companies, and allied nations.
- **Transparency as strategy.** Ukraine publicly documents the attacks it receives, along with attacker tactics and malware, to strengthen collective resilience and build international support.

## How Ukraine Compares
| Country | Strategic Focus | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Deterrence and persistent engagement | Attribution, punishment, alliances |
| Russia | Espionage, coercion, disruption | Covert, with plausible deniability |
| China | Asymmetric deterrence against stronger rivals | Covert intelligence gathering |
| **Ukraine** | **Survival and resilience** | **Defense, transparency, partnerships** |

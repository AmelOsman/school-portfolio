# Ukraine: Resiliency Under Fire
**Amel Osman**

## Ukraine's Cyber Strategy
Compared to the major powers we reviewed, Ukraine brings a different type of cyber strategy. America's strategy focuses on deterrence and engagement with adversary networks. Russia relies on espionage, subversion, and covert coercion to disrupt and manipulate. China's cyber strategy focuses on building asymmetric deterrent strengths to minimize the power gap against stronger rivals. Ukraine's approach can be described as an adaptive posture built on resilience, transparency, and public-private integration. One analyst described Ukraine as a "live-fire space" for other states' cyberweapons (Cerulus, 2019). This makes Ukraine a valuable counterpoint: its strategy is driven by the need for survival rather than the pursuit of advantage.

## Strategy Formation: Attacks as Catalyst
Beginning in November 2013, cyber incidents against Ukrainian institutions became consistent and persistent, long before the full-scale invasion of 2022 (Brantly & Brantly, 2024). This pushed Ukraine's cyber posture from gradual change to urgent action. Before the 2014 presidential vote, Russian-linked intrusions disabled the network of Ukraine's Central Election Commission. In December 2015, BlackEnergy malware, delivered through spear-phishing, caused a blackout affecting 230,000 Ukrainians. It became the first cyberattack to cause a blackout on an energy grid at scale (Cerulus, 2019). Then came the NotPetya attack in 2017, which caused about $10 billion in global damage as it spread from a Ukrainian tax-software vendor to firms worldwide (Cerulus, 2019). These attacks also served as a catalyst. In response, Kyiv was forced to build its institutional cyber defense infrastructure, which led to the development of the State Cyber Protection Center and CERT-UA.

## Defining Features
A defining feature of Ukraine's cyber defense is its close partnership among government, private industry, and international allies. Officials worked alongside cybersecurity firms while receiving support from organizations such as USAID and companies including Microsoft, Cisco, and CrowdStrike. Countries such as Estonia and Lithuania helped strengthen Ukraine's critical infrastructure (Cerulus, 2019).

Another defining feature is transparency. In January 2024, CERT-UA documented 96 cyberattacks involving 44 threat actors and 38 malware variants (Brantly & Brantly, 2024). Ukraine shared this documentation publicly, along with the attackers' tactics and malware, with the goals of strengthening resilience and encouraging international cooperation, all while exposing the state-backed and hacktivist groups involved in the conflict (Brantly & Brantly, 2024).

## Comparison with the United States
The 2018 U.S. National Cyber Strategy emphasizes deterrence through attribution, punishment, and cooperation with allies (National Cyber Strategy, 2018). However, scholars argue that cyberspace is better suited to persistent engagement than to traditional deterrence (Fischerkeller & Harknett, 2017). Although Ukraine lacks the offensive capability needed to deter adversary behavior, it focuses on defending against, documenting, and publishing attacks to strengthen resilience and gain support worldwide.

## Comparison with Russia
Russia's priorities for cyber operations are espionage, coercion, and disruption, while attempting to maintain plausible deniability (Jensen, Valeriano, & Maness, 2019; Wilde & Sherman, 2023). Ukraine's strategy, by contrast, emphasizes defense, resilience, and transparency. In this conflict, cyber activities support conventional warfare rather than replacing it (Brantly & Brantly, 2024).

## Comparison with China
China uses cyber capabilities to deter stronger rivals, gather intelligence, and narrow technological gaps through covert operations (Hjortdal, 2011). Although Ukraine is also the weaker power, it follows the opposite approach: instead of relying on secrecy, it openly publicizes cyberattacks to secure international assistance.

## Conclusion
Ukraine's cyber strategy is shaped by survival rather than power projection. Unlike the United States, Russia, or China, it relies on defense, transparency, and international partnerships instead of deterrence, coercion, or covert cyber operations. Its experience has made Ukraine both a target and a testing ground for modern cyber conflict (Cerulus, 2019).
